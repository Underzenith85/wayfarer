# Physical extra-effort consequences

Selected sources: Characters third printing B123 and Campaigns fourth printing
B356–357/B422. The exact PDF hashes are the repository's selected baseline.

A failed extra-effort roll still pays its source FP cost. Critical success waives
the extra-effort cost; ordinary task costs remain. Critical failure applies actual
canonical HP injury equal to the effort and ordinary task FP cost. A natural 18
also draws the immediate HT avoidance roll; failure records a typed lasting
condition and its 1d-month deadline, or permanent injury on critical failure.
The trusted task author chooses the relevant leg/foot or back construction.

The canonical reducer handles internal muscle injury at its authored location
without armor or the attack limb HP cap. It preserves shock, major-wound, death,
crippling and deterministic check records. Localized exertion cannot destroy or
sever a limb. Back injuries from digging/lifting retain a rest-only HP debt;
bandaging/first aid cannot remove it. Real natural healing retires that debt.

Current canonical FP must match the actor projection. Machines and incapacitated
actors reject before dice. Nonpositive FP uses the existing continued-exertion
procedure: a failed Will check persists collapse without applying a second effort
cost or completing the task. Costs and chosen consequences use one source-sized
interval per command, not arbitrary repeated rolls hidden in one command.

This contributes to #769. Full Bad Back risk/throw-back consumers, short hiking
march authoring and end-of-march settlement require further integration before
all task variants are verified. Optional combat extra effort is excluded by the
issue. No source or profile certification status is promoted.

Tests inspect HP/FP, canonical crippling, the natural-18 HT outcome, lasting and
permanent state, rest-only healing, fatigue penalties, collapse, authority, exact
retry and command-ID conflicts. The legacy descriptive consequence remains an
annotation; it no longer substitutes for injury or condition state.
